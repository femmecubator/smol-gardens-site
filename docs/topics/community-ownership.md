# Summary

:::tip Challenge

What concrete veto power or governance mechanism does the community hold over
your tool once it is deployed in the wild?

:::

## Why it matters

People affected by an AI system rarely get a say in how it is built. Decisions
about what data to collect, which outcomes to optimise for and what counts as
an acceptable error are usually made by a small team, far from the people who
live with the results.

Community governance gives affected people a **standing role** in those
decisions — not a one-off survey at launch, but a mechanism that keeps running
for as long as the system does. Civic tech is built in the name of the public;
governance is how the public gets to hold it to that promise.

## Principles

1. **Involve people early.** Bring the community in while the problem is still
   being defined, not after the model is trained.
2. **Share real power.** Advice that can be ignored is not governance. Agree up
   front which decisions the community can block or change.
3. **Make it continuous.** Systems drift, data changes and new harms appear.
   Review on a schedule, not only when something goes wrong.
4. **Lower the cost of taking part.** Pay people for their time, meet where
   they already are, and explain the system without jargon.
5. **Close the loop.** Report back on what changed because of community input —
   and what did not, and why.

## What it looks like in practice

- A community advisory group that reviews major changes before they ship
- A public channel for reporting harm, with a committed response time
- Plain-language reports on how the system is used and where it fails
- A clear way to opt out, and to have personal data removed
- A sunset plan: who decides when the system should be retired

:::tip Questions to ask your team

- Who is affected by this system but has no say in how it works?
- What would it take for them to stop it if it caused harm?
- When did we last change something because a community member asked?

:::

:::note

Governance takes time and money. Budget for it from the start, the same way you
would for hosting or security — retrofitting it after launch costs more.

:::
